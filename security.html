Making eBuzz hard to hack, and quick to recover

There is no such thing as a hacker-proof application. Every system with users, money and third-party code has a way in. What a serious security programme buys is a different outcome: attacks that fail against a control rather than succeed quietly, attacks that are detected in minutes rather than months, and a breach that costs a weekend instead of the company. This page is the working plan for that — every class of vulnerability that applies to eBuzz with its fix, what we watch, and exactly what happens in the first hour of a breach.

Five things that decide the outcome

Most of the security value in a product like this comes from a small number of structural decisions, taken early. Everything else is maintenance.

The honest framing for investors and the board: we are not claiming eBuzz cannot be breached. We are claiming that the high-value paths — customer data, payouts and the agent fleet — each require an attacker to defeat several independent controls, that we will know quickly when someone tries, and that we have rehearsed what we do next. That is what "secure" means for a real business.

Recovery targets

Recovery point objective is how much data we can afford to lose. Recovery time objective is how long the service can be gone. Both are tested quarterly, because a backup that has never been restored is a hypothesis.

Afterwards

The programme, in order

Sequenced so the expensive-to-retrofit decisions happen first. Year-one budget is roughly $420k: people $260k, tooling $90k, pentest and audits $70k — about 8% of the seed round, which is the right weight for a marketplace whose product is trust.

Who owns it

A fractional CISO from month 0 and a full-time Security Lead by month 4. Risk & Compliance meets monthly; an AI governance board signs off every change to agent autonomy.

How it is tested

Scans on every PR, DAST weekly, third-party pentest before launch and annually, AI red-teaming quarterly, and a public bug bounty three months after launch.
